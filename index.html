Why I do this

Your trade, my whole job.

I didn't get into this to build websites for software startups. I got into it because I kept meeting people who were excellent at something real. Hardwood floors. Brickwork. Pest control. And then watching them lose online to companies who were worse at the job.

It's a fixable problem, and it usually goes unfixed for years. Most owners I talk to have already been quoted five figures by an agency that wanted a twelve-month retainer on top of it. Or sold a four-hundred-dollar template by somebody who never asked what they actually do. After that, giving up on the whole idea is a pretty reasonable response. It's just not the only one.

So that's the business. Local trades and home services, one client at a time, built by whoever you talked to on the phone. Which is me. There's no account manager and no handoff to a team you never meet.

Why it's only trades

I design for the trades, not for design awards.

Most studios have never set foot on a job site. I build for one kind of business and nothing else, so I already know how a homeowner picks a contractor and what makes them call instead of closing the tab.

That's the whole advantage. Every decision on the page gets made against how your customers actually buy, which is why none of this looks like the template every other contractor in your area is running.

  • You don't have to explain your own trade to me. No walking me through the difference between refinishing and recoating, or why a builder and a homeowner need different forms.
  • I design for a phone in a work glove. Tap targets, click-to-call, and pages that load on one bar of signal.
  • I know what makes someone call. Licenses, years in business, real job photos, a straight answer on price. Placed where they actually get read.
  • One person, start to finish. Whoever picks up the phone is the person designing it and the person building it. That's me.

A few at a time, so each one gets done properly.
